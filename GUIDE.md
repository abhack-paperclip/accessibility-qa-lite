# A 15-Minute Accessibility QA Workflow for Digital Content

A practical pre-release check can catch many common accessibility problems before your content reaches learners, customers, or colleagues.

This guide is a lightweight workflow aid, not a compliance certification or legal opinion.

## 1. Pick representative content

Do not attempt to inspect an entire course or content library at once.

Choose a representative page, document, lesson, or workflow that includes the kinds of structures you publish regularly.

## 2. Check the document structure

Look at headings, lists, links, and reading order.

Ask:
- Do headings form a logical hierarchy?
- Are lists represented as lists?
- Does the content make sense when read linearly?
- Do links communicate their destination or purpose?

## 3. Check images and media

For meaningful images, check that equivalent information is available through alternative text or nearby text.

For media with speech, check that captions are available.

For decorative visuals, avoid forcing users to process unnecessary alternative text.

## 4. Try keyboard-only navigation

Put the mouse aside.

Tab through the representative content and interaction points.

Check whether:
- important controls can be reached
- the current focus is visible
- focus moves in a sensible order
- keyboard focus becomes trapped anywhere

## 5. Check text presentation

Enlarge or zoom the content.

Look for:
- text or controls that become inaccessible
- content that overlaps or disappears
- information communicated only through color
- poor link identification

## 6. Check tables and complex content

Make sure table headers are identifiable and that complex structures have a sensible reading experience.

For documents and PDFs, check tags, headings, and reading order where applicable.

## 7. Record issues immediately

A finding that is not recorded tends to become a finding that survives the next release.

For each issue, capture:
- location
- description
- priority
- owner
- status
- evidence
- resolution

## 8. Make a release decision

After the smoke test, record what was checked and what remains unresolved.

Use the same workflow each release so QA becomes repeatable instead of dependent on memory.
